# Navigator validation protocol

**Status: a protocol, written before the study it describes. Nothing here has been
submitted, registered or run except where a result is stated.** It is meant to be
registered (for example on the Open Science Framework) before any clinician rating
begins, so that the bars cannot move afterwards.

## What is being validated

`backend/navigator.py` with `backend/navigator_rules.json`: a rule engine that reads
symptoms, a few facts and lab results, and says whether the combination meets a published
guideline threshold for a prompt check. The rules are transcribed from the 2015 edition of
the NICE suspected-cancer guideline (NG12). It is not a prediction model and has no
training data.

Because it only applies written rules, three different things can be wrong with it, and
each needs its own test.

| Question | What could be wrong | How it is tested |
|---|---|---|
| **Fidelity** | The rule file does not say what the guideline says | Clinician review of the rule file against the current guideline, then a vignette study |
| **Burden** | It alerts so many people that it is unusable | Run on a general-population sample (done, below) |
| **Readability** | A person without medical training cannot follow it | Reading-level measurement (done, below), then readers |

The one thing this protocol does not test is whether the guideline is right. That is
outside its scope and is stated as such.

## Already done, on public data

**Superseded in part:** the larger, pre-registered evidence study (37,915 adults, four eras, outcomes, ferritin) is in `docs/NAVIGATOR_EVIDENCE_PREREG.md`, `experiments/navigator_evidence.py` and the paper in `docs/paper/`. The 13,295-person burden table below is the earlier, smaller version.

### 1. Alert burden: `experiments/navigator_lab_trigger_burden.py`

The real engine was run on 13,295 NHANES adults aged 40 and over with a blood count, with
no symptoms entered, to see what lab results alone would trigger.

| Age | Told "see a doctor soon" | Told "worth raising" | Any alert |
|---|---|---|---|
| 40 to 49 | 0.0% | 3.8% | 3.8% |
| 50 to 59 | 0.0% | 1.4% | 1.4% |
| 60 to 69 | 1.7% | 5.9% | 7.6% |
| 70 to 79 | 1.5% | 10.5% | 12.0% |
| 80 and over | 0.9% | 16.3% | 17.2% |

Two rules drive almost all of it: anaemia from 60 (a stool test) and small-celled anaemia
from 60 (an urgent referral). **A design decision came out of this.** The guideline's lung
rule lists a high platelet count as a trigger, and applied literally it alerted 3.7% of
symptom-free adults over 40. It is gated behind at least one symptom and recorded in the
rule file as a deviation a clinician must confirm.

Limits: NHANES has no symptoms and records death rather than diagnosis, so this measures
workload and does not validate any alert. Iron deficiency is inferred from small red cells
because the cohort has no ferritin, which over-alerts relative to a confirmed definition.

### 2. Readability: `experiments/navigator_readability.py`

A bar was set before looking: every patient-facing action line at or below US grade 10 and
the average at or below grade 8. The first run missed it. Five action lines were over grade
10 and the emergency advice, the most important text on the page, scored grade 16.5 because
it was one long sentence. After rewriting, the action and headline lines average grade 6.9
with none over 10, and the emergency advice scores about grade 5. This is a reading-level
floor and not a measure of understanding.

## To do: the fidelity study (needs clinicians)

**Question.** Does the tool reach the same recommendation as a clinician applying the
guideline text to the same patient description?

**Design.** A set of written patient vignettes, each rated independently by clinicians who
have not seen the tool's output, then compared with the tool.

- **Vignettes.** About 120, built so that every rule is tested near its boundary: one
  vignette that just meets each threshold, one that just misses it (age 39 against 40,
  frequency 11 against 12 days a month, ferritin 14 against 16), plus symptom-free and
  multi-symptom patients. Generated by a script from the rule file so the set is
  reproducible, then edited by a clinician for realism.
- **Raters.** At least three clinicians, rating independently and blind to the tool.
  Each gives one of the tool's outputs: see a doctor soon, worth raising, mention at a
  routine visit, or no threshold met, with the site of concern.
- **Reference standard.** The guideline's own text. Where the three raters disagree, a
  fourth adjudicates and the guideline is quoted.

**Primary outcome.** Agreement between the tool and the adjudicated reference on the
four-way output, as unweighted Cohen's kappa with a 95% interval.

**Bars, set now.** The tool is called faithful only if kappa is at least 0.90 against the
adjudicated reference AND every disagreement is explained and fixed or recorded as a
deliberate deviation. Any disagreement that would make the tool under-alert (say less
urgent than the reference) is counted separately and must be zero in the final version,
because that is the harmful direction.

**Why the harmful direction is separate.** A tool that over-alerts annoys people. One that
under-alerts reassures someone who should be seen.

## To do: clinician sign-off of the rules

Before any vignette study, a clinician reads `navigator_rules.json` against the current
guideline and records, for every rule, one of: matches, needs change, or remove. The file
has a `review_status` and `reviewed_by` field that stay at "not yet reviewed" until this is
done, and the tool displays that status to every user.

Open questions for that review, from the rule file:

1. The lung chest X-ray rule gated behind a symptom (a deviation from the literal text).
2. Whether lab-only alerts should be shown to symptom-free people at all.
3. Every rule against the current guideline: the 2015 edition has been revised, for
   example the bowel pathway now uses the faecal immunochemical test.
4. Whether US users need US guidance in place of or alongside this UK guidance.

## To do: comprehension

Reading level is not understanding. A small study where lay readers read three outputs and
say, in their own words, what they would do next would test it. If it recruits people it is
human-subjects research and needs the institution's ethics determination first.

## What is not claimed

That it finds cancer. That it has been tested on patients. That the guideline rules are
right. That reading level equals comprehension. That any result here applies to the United
States before US guidance is reconciled.

## What a result would let the paper say

*Development and pre-specified validation of a patient-facing symptom-and-laboratory
navigator for cancer referral thresholds: rule fidelity, alert burden and readability.* It
is a real, checkable, self-contained study with a clinician indispensable to it, and its
honest limitation, that it applies a guideline and does not discover one, is stated in its
first paragraph.
