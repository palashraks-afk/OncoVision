# Does a lay reader understand what the tool says? A protocol

**Status:** a plan, not a study. Nothing has been run and nobody has been recruited. This
involves people, so it needs an ethics determination before any recruiting (see "Before you
start"). Reading level was measured automatically (grade 6.9); this is the test the number
cannot replace.

## Question

When an adult with no medical training reads the tool's answer, can they say in their own words
what it told them, what to do next, and what it did not say?

## Design

Within-person, one session of about 15 minutes. Each reader sees three fixed example outputs
(printed or on screen) and answers five short questions after each, in their own words. A
second person scores the answers against a key.

### The three outputs (fixed text, written before recruiting)

1. **Urgent-tier.** The invented case in the paper: a 66-year-old who has smoked, with weight
   loss and iron-deficiency anaemia ("see a doctor soon" for bowel and lung).
2. **Lab-only.** A 44-year-old woman with low iron and no symptoms, with the periods note and the
   "lab results alone" caveat.
3. **Nothing met.** A 35-year-old with no symptoms and normal labs ("no threshold met").

### Questions after each (open response)

1. In your own words, what is this telling you?
2. What would you do next, if this were you?
3. Does it say you have cancer? (yes / no / can't tell)
4. How urgent does it sound? (0 = not at all, 10 = emergency)
5. Is there anything in it you did not understand?

### Scoring key (set before the first reader)

- **Understood the message:** says the tool found a reason to talk to a doctor (outputs 1 and 2) or found no threshold (output 3).
- **Correct action:** says to see or ask a doctor. For output 3, does not say they are definitely fine.
- **No false diagnosis:** says "no" or "can't tell" to question 3 for all three outputs.
- **Urgency fits:** output 1 rated 6 or more; output 2 rated 1 to 5; output 3 rated 0 to 3.
- **Safe misreading rate:** the share who think output 3 means "nothing is wrong". This is the harmful misreading and is reported on its own.

### Bars, set now

- At least **80%** of readers meet "understood the message" and "correct action" on each output.
- At most **5%** think any output says they have cancer.
- At most **10%** read output 3 as "definitely nothing wrong". If higher, the wording must change and the study repeats.

### Size

Twenty readers, ten of them with no more than a high-school education, give a rough estimate (a
95% interval around 80% with n = 20 is about 58 to 92%). This is a pilot to find wording that
fails, not proof it works. If a wording fails, change it and rerun with new readers.

## Before you start

1. **Ethics.** Ask Dr. Chavan's institution, or your school's research office, whether this needs
   review. It collects no health information from the readers and no names, but asking people
   anything is still human-subjects research, and a determination in writing comes first.
   Under-18 readers need parental consent, and as a student under 18 you should not recruit
   alone; have a supervising adult.
2. **No health advice.** Readers are told the examples are invented and that this is a test of
   the wording, not of them.
3. **Anonymous.** Record an ID, age band and education level only.
4. **Disclosure.** Say what the tool is and that it is a research prototype.

## What it would let the paper say

Either "in a pilot of 20 readers the wording met the bars" or, more usefully, "these three
sentences were misunderstood by N readers and were changed to ...". Both are honest.
